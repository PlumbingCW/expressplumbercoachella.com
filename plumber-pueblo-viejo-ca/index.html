What Pueblo Viejo is, and what we could confirm

Pueblo Viejo is the name Coachella uses for its historic downtown. The city's zoning code calls it the Pueblo Viejo District and sets a downtown zone, labeled DT-PV, for its core. The stated purpose of that zone is a denser, walkable downtown with housing, offices and shops. Palm Springs Life describes the district as a walkable arts and cultural hub, built around the Spanish Revival library and conference center with its clock tower, and the murals along Sixth Street.

The city does not publish a single neighborhood boundary for residents. One section of the zoning code, written to decide where cannabis businesses may locate, describes the district as bounded by Cesar Chavez Street on the south, First Street on the west, Grapefruit Boulevard on the north and Ninth Street on the east. Treat that as a rough guide to where the name applies, not a legal map. If your address is near those edges, call and we will confirm service.

What the city says can be built and lived in here

These points come from the DT-PV chapter of Coachella's zoning code, and they explain why the area has a different plumbing mix than a street of single-family houses.

  • Apartment buildings of five or more units are a permitted use, so many addresses share walls, risers and drain stacks
  • Single-family homes built before July 1, 2019 are allowed to remain, so some older houses still stand downtown
  • Accessory dwelling units are allowed, which means a second kitchen or bath may sit behind a main house
  • Shops and offices are part of the mix, and some food businesses share buildings with apartments

Pueblo Viejo Villas and newer apartment buildings

One recent addition is Pueblo Viejo Villas, a 105-unit apartment community on Sixth Street near the library and city hall. KESQ reported that it offers one-, two- and three-bedroom homes aimed at families, and that the city expected it to be fully occupied within months of its opening. We do not do or claim any work at that property. We mention it because it shows the kind of building going up here: new multi-family construction next to older buildings.

General plumbing information applies in both. A new building and an old one both have shutoff valves, water heaters, drain lines and fixtures, and the first question on any call is the same: where is the water coming from, and who controls the valve that stops it?

Sewer and water in downtown Coachella

Sewer service in the city of Coachella is run by the City's Utilities Department, listed as the Coachella Water Authority and Sanitary District. A blockage in the City main under the street is the City's to clear, while a blockage in the pipe that serves your building is the owner's.

A useful test is to ask neighbors. If several buildings on the same block back up at once, the problem may be in the City main, and the Utilities Department lists a sewer emergency standby line at (760) 625-6781. If only your building backs up, call us for sewer line cleaning or a camera check. We do not clear City mains.

Before we arrive: a leak or backup in a shared building

In an apartment or mixed-use building, a few quick steps protect you and your neighbors.

1

Find your own shutoff

Look under the sink, behind the toilet and at the water heater. Close the valve that serves the leaking fixture.

2

Tell the building contact

Call the property manager or owner if the water cannot be stopped at your unit. They control the building valves.

3

Stop using drains

If a drain is backing up, stop running sinks, showers and washers. Water has nowhere to go and may surface in a lower unit.

4

Call the line

Give the exact address, the unit, what you see and what you have already closed.

Plumbing jobs that suit downtown buildings

In a building with several units or tenants, the same few jobs come up as anywhere. A slow kitchen or bathroom line is a case for drain cleaning. A damp wall or ceiling, or a water bill that jumps with no change in use, calls for leak detection before anyone opens a wall. A tank that has stopped heating is a case for water heater repair, and a restaurant or shop with heavy daily water use may need commercial plumbing.

We do not know the condition of the pipes in any particular downtown building, and we do not assume it from age. A camera, a pressure check or a look at the fixtures tells us more than a date on a deed.
