Emergency toilet repair: stop the water first

Toilet repair in Coachella, CA covers clogs, leaks at the base, running tanks, failed fill valves, cracked bowls and replacement. A toilet that is overflowing or leaking at the base is an emergency because water spreads to flooring quickly. Our 24/7 line is open at night and on weekends.

Turn the small valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the supply. If the bowl is overflowing, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop more water entering. Mop up and do not flush again until a plumber checks the line.

Common toilet problems

Running toilet

A worn flapper or fill valve leaks into the bowl and wastes water.

Leak at the base

A failed wax ring or loose flange. Do not ignore it, since water can rot the floor.

Weak flush

Clogged rim jets or a partial blockage in the trap.

Rocking toilet

A loose or damaged flange, or an uneven floor.

Repeated clogs

A line problem rather than a toilet problem. See drain cleaning.

Repair or replace?

A fill valve, flapper or wax ring is a small repair. A cracked bowl, an old high-flow unit or a toilet that has needed repeated repairs may be better replaced. Modern toilets use less water per flush, which matters when your water bill includes a tiered rate.

Installation involves removing the old unit, checking the flange, setting a new wax ring or seal, leveling and connecting the supply. A good installer tests the flush and checks for leaks. If your flange is corroded or broken, the plumber will tell you before setting the toilet.

Water use and toilets in the desert

Water is a precious resource in the Coachella Valley, and the Coachella Valley Water District promotes conservation. A leaking flapper wastes water silently and shows up as a higher bill. If your meter runs when nothing is on, test your toilet with a few drops of food coloring in the tank.

If the color shows up in the bowl without flushing, the flapper leaks. A cheap part, but it can waste a surprising amount of water over a month. Plumbing work in California is checked against the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). It covers fixture installation, which is why a plumber checks the flange and supply connection before setting a new toilet.
